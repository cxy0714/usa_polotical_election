## What this archive covers

This case documents the 2026 United States Senate election in Michigan: an open Class 2 seat vacated by Gary Peters' retirement, a three-candidate Democratic primary won narrowly by Abdul El-Sayed on August 4, 2026, an uncontested Republican primary won by Mike Rogers, and a general election on November 3, 2026 that El-Sayed won 52.54% to 45.46% [SRC-0001]. It covers both candidates' biographies, positions, schedules, speeches, imagery, advertising, coverage, money and the surrounding electoral context, over 286 CSV rows and 87 registered sources. It contains no HTML and no website build.

## Five structural facts this archive establishes

**1. The winner lost the Democratic base.** El-Sayed carried 30 of Michigan's 83 counties while losing Wayne, Oakland and Macomb — the three counties that hold most of the Democratic vote — and Stevens took Detroit with more than 60% [SRC-0008]. The general election was therefore the first Democratic statewide win in Michigan without those three counties since 2016 [SRC-0001]. Any account of this race that assumes a normal Democratic coalition is wrong on the arithmetic.

**2. The primary's deciding margin was smaller than the withdrawn candidate's vote.** El-Sayed beat Stevens by 13,792 votes, while Mallory McMorrow — who had suspended her campaign on July 5 — took 61,516 votes, 4.5 times the margin [SRC-0001] [SRC-0033]. The residual vote of a candidate who was no longer campaigning was larger than the gap that decided the nomination.

**3. Outside money outweighed both campaigns combined.** The two committees raised $76,847,311 between them through October 14, 2026 ($53,830,872 for El-Sayed, $23,016,439 for Rogers) [SRC-0001]. Outside spending in the same contest ran to roughly $62 million in the primary alone plus $135.7 million in the general election, about 2.6 times the candidates' combined receipts [SRC-0002] [SRC-0073]. The Detroit News' $243 million total makes this the most expensive Senate race in Michigan history [SRC-0073].

**4. County count and vote share moved in opposite directions.** Rogers carried 53 of 83 counties and lost by 7.08 points; El-Sayed carried 30 and won. Five counties flipped Republican-to-Democratic relative to 2024 — Grand Traverse, Isabella, Kent, Leelanau and Muskegon [SRC-0001].

**5. One candidate did most of the debating.** El-Sayed appeared in seven debates and forums; Rogers in two joint debates and one policy forum, and he declined the September 15 Fox 2 invitation on the morning of the broadcast, which proceeded with an empty podium [SRC-0049] [SRC-0019] [SRC-0020]. Six hours of El-Sayed speaking to camera in the general election have no Rogers counterpart in the record.
