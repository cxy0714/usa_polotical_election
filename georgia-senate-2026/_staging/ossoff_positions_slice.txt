 Political positions
 [ edit ] 
 Ossoff delivers victory speech after winning re-election, November 2026 
 In 2017, The New Yorker wrote that Ossoff has "progressive positions on women's issues and health care" and "moderate stances on jobs and security". [ 7 ] Matthew Yglesias of Vox also called Ossoff a moderate, and called his 2017 run for office an " Obama -style campaign." [ 122 ] According to The Washington Post , Ossoff's 2017 campaign opted "not to turn the special election into a referendum on Trump's alleged scandals" but to focus on "policy decisions by the president and congressional Republicans". [ 123 ] The Atlanta Journal-Constitution wrote that he "often tried to avoid nationalizing that campaign over fears of losing moderate voters". [ 124 ] The New York Times observed that his 2017 campaign distanced itself from the national Democratic Party. [ 125 ] 
 In 2020, The Atlanta Journal-Constitution wrote that Ossoff was "more unapologetic about embracing liberal policy ideas than his Democratic predecessors during past statewide races. And where he once hesitated to hit Trump directly, he now pulls no punches as he seeks to tie Perdue to his White House ally." [ 126 ] 
 In 2023, the Lugar Center ranked Ossoff 33rd among U.S. senators for bipartisanship. [ 127 ] In 2025, The Associated Press wrote: "There's the Jon Ossoff who built his political career around criticizing Donald Trump. There's also the Ossoff who works with Republicans, advancing the interests of Georgia's farmers and military bases." [ 128 ] 
 Abortion
 [ edit ] 
 Ossoff supports abortion rights . [ 129 ] He pledged to support only judges who would uphold Roe v. Wade , and he also supports Planned Parenthood . [ 130 ] In response to the June 2022 overturning of Roe v. Wade , Ossoff said the U.S. Supreme Court had "stripped American women of autonomy over their most personal health care decisions". [ 131 ] 
 Cannabis legalization
 [ edit ] 
 Ossoff has said, "It's past time to legalize, regulate, and tax cannabis – whose prohibition only enriches cartels, bail bondsmen, and the owners of private prisons". [ 132 ] He has said that he will push for "nationwide legalization" of cannabis, calling it "much less dangerous than alcohol". [ 133 ]

 Ossoff supports the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act (Obamacare). [ 49 ] [ 166 ] His health care policy includes three principles: "One, no American should suffer or die from preventable or treatable illness. Two, no one should go broke because they get sick. And three, no business should go under or lay off employees because it can't keep up with health insurance premiums." [ 167 ] He does not support a single-payer health care system, such as Medicare for All . [ 125 ] [ 136 ] He opposed both the March 2017 and May 2017 versions of the American Health Care Act , the House Republican bill that would have repealed the Affordable Care Act. [ 168 ] He said the May 2017 version was worse than the earlier one "because it does even less to protect those with preexisting conditions." [ 169 ] 
 Ossoff told the Atlanta Journal-Constitution that he supported tax credits for small businesses related to health care. [ 170 ] [ 171 ] 
 In 2020, Ossoff declared support for a public health insurance option . [ 172 ] 
 Immigration
 [ edit ] 
 Ossoff supports comprehensive immigration reform that would both strengthen enforcement along the Mexican border and provide a path to citizenship for some undocumented immigrants . [ 170 ] 
 After the murder of Laken Riley , a Georgia nursing student killed by an undocumented immigrant, Ossoff criticized Donald Trump for his efforts to derail the passage of a bipartisan border security bill . [ 173 ] In 2025, Ossoff was one of 12 Democratic senators who joined all Republicans in voting for the Laken Riley Act . [ 174 ] 
 LGBTQ rights
 [ edit ] 
 Ossoff has called his support for the LGBTQ community "unwavering". [ 175 ] He also supports the Equality Act , which would prohibit discrimination on the basis of sex, gender identity, or sexual orientation. [ 176 ] 
 First Trump administration
 [ edit ] 
 Ossoff has been sharply critical of President Donald Trump , criticizing what he calls Trump's "divisive approach to government" [ 177 ] and saying: "I have great respect for the office. I don't have great personal admiration for the man himself." [ 178 ] On April 19, 2017, the day before Ossoff's primary, Trump called him a "super Liberal Democrat" who wanted to "protect criminals, allow illegal immigration and raise taxes" in a tweet . Ossoff dismissed Trump's claims and called him "misinformed". [ 179 ] FactCheck.org found that Trump's claim was a distortion and that there was no evidence that Ossoff had ever advocated for any broad-based tax hikes. [ 170 ] Nevertheless, Ossoff said that he would be willing to work with Trump on issues of mutual interest, such as infrastructure spending. [ 178 ] 
 After Trump's disclosure of classified information to Russia , Ossoff said of impeachment, "I don't think we're there." [ 180 ] He called for "a full and transparent and independent assessment of what level of interference there was by Russian intelligence services in the U.S. election. And overseers in Congress and any independent counsel or commission to do so should follow those facts wherever they lead." [ 180 ] 
 Ossoff voted to convict Trump during his second impeachment on the charge of incitement of insurrection following the 2021 United States Capitol attack , joining all Democrats and seven Republicans. [ 181 ] 
 Voting rights
 [ edit ] 
 Ossoff supports passage of the John Lewis Voting Rights Act . [ 182 ] [ 183 ] 
 After Georgia's Election Integrity Act of 2021 passed the state legislature and was signed into law by Governor Brian Kemp , several businesses, from the Major League Baseball organization to the production of the Will Smith film Emancipation , boycotted Georgia in protest. [ 184 ] In an interview, Ossoff expressed his displeasure with the bill while also saying he did not support the corporate boycotts because Georgians "rely upon and hope for and welcome jobs, investment, and opportunity". [ 185 ] 
 Public image
 [ edit ] 
 Ossoff has been described as able to effectively appeal to young people by using TikTok , a social media app popular with Generation Z . [ 186 ] On the night he was elected to the Senate, Ossoff's posts on Twitter from the previous decade, which included several references to Star Wars , the musical group Imagine Dragons , and anime , attracted renewed attention on social media. [ 187 ] He has been called the "first Extremely Online senator". [ 188 ] 
 In January 2021, Vogue reported on an "adoring" Instagram account of self-declared " simps " expressing affection for Ossoff. [ 189 ] In July 2021, The Daily Beast reported on an "Ossimp Patrol" on Twitter that monitored "Ossoff simps" on the platform and replied to their tweets with an ActBlue link prompting them to donate to Raphael Warnock 's 2022 reelection campaign and get out the vote organizations in Georgia and Texas . [ 190 ] When shown this by The Daily Beast , Ossoff is said to have "paused for a moment" and "furrowed his brow" before saying he was not aware of any of it, but commended the "great community" that supported him during his 2020–2021 Senate campaign . [ 190 ]
