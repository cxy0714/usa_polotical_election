Brown won by 222,605 votes, a margin of 4.84 percentage points. The four vote counts sum exactly to the reported total, which is the strongest internal check available without the official canvass.

Rounding, and one documented conflict

The printed percentages sum to 100.01, not 100.00: 50.01 + 45.17 + 2.79 + 2.04. That is ordinary rounding of four shares that each carry two decimals, not an error in the counts.

Two shares disagree between sources. The Calfee newsletter cited by the race article prints Husted at 45.15% and Levy at 2.05%; the article's election box prints 45.17% and 2.04%. Recomputed from the vote counts, Husted is 2,080,064 / 4,604,860 = 45.171% and Levy is 93,786 / 4,604,860 = 2.037%. The vote counts are the primary fact and the computed shares match the article's box, so 45.17 and 2.04 are retained, with the Calfee figures recorded as conflicting sources in election_results.csv.

Turnout and early voting

Nearly 4.56 million Ohioans voted. More than 1.7 million voted early, about 17% more than in 2022, and the early electorate split roughly 468,000 Democratic, 432,000 Republican and 807,000 unaffiliated SRC-1182. The unaffiliated share of early votes is the single most interesting number in the file: it is larger than the two party blocs combined.

The county picture, and the gap

There is no county-results table in this archive. The race article publishes two county-level map graphics (one for the general election, one for the Democratic primary) but no table of county vote totals, and the Ohio Secretary of State's canvass spreadsheets returned HTTP 403 to this archiver. county_results.csv is therefore header-only, with zero rows, and that is a documented gap rather than an omission.

What the maps do support is a list of seven counties that flipped from Republican to Democratic between the 2024 presidential result and this Senate result: Delaware, Erie, Lake, Mahoning, Portage, Trumbull and Wood. Those seven names are recorded here and in research_gaps.md; the underlying county totals are not.

The primary results

Brown 712,106 (89.40%) to Kincaid 84,405 (10.60%) on 2026-05-05; Husted unopposed with 736,413. The 796,511 Democratic primary votes are 17% of the general-election turnout, which is a normal drop-off for a non-competitive primary.

Dates that are corrected here

Husted's appointment and oath are dated 2025-01-17 and 2025-01-21 respectively SRC-1016SRC-1178. The brief's alternative dates (2025-01-10, 2025-01-14) are rejected; see election_context.md.

Contested characterisations

The result was read two ways in the coverage. Cleveland.com's analysis framed it as a personal rebuke of Husted and of his corporate ties SRC-1076; other coverage framed it as Brown's return to a seat he had held for 18 years. Both readings are recorded in media_coverage.csv as framing, not as findings.
