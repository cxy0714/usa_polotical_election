Campaign finance notes

The two reporting points

The archive holds two snapshots, both taken from the FEC-derived tables in the race article.

As of 2026-04-18 (period ending 2026-03-31): Brown had raised $24,471,408, spent $7,956,946 and held $16,514,461. Husted had raised $10,257,823, spent $2,017,868 and held $8,239,954. The same table lists the Democratic primary field: Ode $5,001,938 raised, Kincaid $41,804, Volpe $4,337.

As of 2026-10-14 (period ending 2026-09-30): Brown $68,409,200 raised, $63,956,397 spent, $4,452,803 on hand; Husted $19,894,786 raised, $15,655,689 spent, $4,239,097 on hand; Redpath $55,256 / $47,000 / $8,255; Levy $132,712 / $107,925 / $26,916.

What the columns mean, and what they do not

The source tables print three numbers per candidate. individual_contributions, pac_contributions and debts_owed are therefore unknown for every row rather than estimated from the totals. The receipts figure at the October point is cumulative for the cycle, not a single quarter's intake, and the notes column says so.

The outside-money gap

Candidate committees are the small part of the picture. AdImpact's estimate, reported by the Ohio Capital Journal, puts outside-group spending above $325 million in this race — several times the combined candidate totals. That money flows through committees that file separately, and none of it appears in campaign_finance.csv. A user who reads only the candidate table will understate total spending by an order of magnitude.

The spending pattern

Brown raised roughly 3.4 times Husted's total and had spent 96% of what he raised by 2026-09-30, entering the final month with $4.45 million on hand — about the same as Husted's $4.24 million. The near-identical cash position at the end, against a 3.4x fundraising gap, is the most striking number in the file.
