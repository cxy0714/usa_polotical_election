# [8] General election

General election
Cost of living, the economy, foreign wars, and the candidates' experience were the central issues for voters.

The seat was initially considered safe for Republicans as South Carolina is normally considered a strongly red state at the state and federal levels, but after Graham said during a primary runoff debate that "national security is not my thing,” some voters began to see her as inexperienced and unqualified, inflaming a divide between establishment and conservative Republicans who objected to Trump's involvement in the race, and the race became a contested tossup. A The New York Times/Siena poll found that "51 percent of the voters in the polls, including 18 percent of Republicans" said Graham "did not have the right experience to be an effective senator". In contrast, "54 percent of voters, including 22 percent of Republicans, said that Dr. Andrews did."
