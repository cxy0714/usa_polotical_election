Annie Elizabeth Andrews (; born August 9, 1980) is an American pediatrician and politician who is the United States senator-elect from South Carolina. A member of the Democratic Party, she defeated Republican incumbent Darline Graham in the 2026 United States Senate election, becoming the first woman elected to the Senate from South Carolina and the first Democrat to win a Senate election in South Carolina since 1998. She will assume office on January 3, 2027.

Born in Paducah, Kentucky and raised in Indianapolis, Andrews received her Doctor of Medicine from the University of Cincinnati and a master's degree in clinical research from Medical University of South Carolina. She worked as a pediatric hospitalist at MUSC Shawn Jenkins Children's Hospital and was a professor of pediatrics at MUSC. Her medical career included research and advocacy concerning firearm injuries among children. Andrews was the Democratic nominee for South Carolina's 1st congressional district in the 2022 election, losing to incumbent Representative Nancy Mace.

Early life and education
Annie Elizabeth Lintzenich was born on August 9, 1980 in Paducah, Kentucky, and grew up in Indianapolis, where she graduated from Cathedral High School. She received her bachelor's degree from the University of Dayton, where she was a member of Chi Omega sorority. She went on to earn her master's degree in clinical research from the Medical University of South Carolina, and her Doctor of Medicine degree from the University of Cincinnati. She also completed a master's degree in clinical research after starting work at the MUSC Shawn Jenkins Children's Hospital.

Medical career
Andrews completed her medical residency in pediatrics in Cincinnati, Ohio, before moving to South Carolina's Lowcountry in 2009. She worked for 14 years at MUSC Children's Hospital in Charleston, as a pediatric hospitalist. She announced in September 2022, while running for Congress, that she was taking an unpaid leave "to protect myself and my colleagues". She resigned in June 2023.

As of mid-2025, Andrews worked part-time as a pediatrician in Children's National Hospital in Washington, D.C., but lived in Charleston.

Andrews became interested in political activism after the Parkland high school shooting in 2018, which convinced her to join the gun safety advocacy group Moms Demand Action. She began doing clinical research into gun violence prevention to reduce the number of pediatric injuries from firearms. She described gun violence prevention as directly related to her work as a pediatrician. She announced her candidacy for South Carolina's 1st congressional district in November 2021; it was her first time running for political office. She won the Democratic nomination in June 2022 after running unopposed.

In September, the incumbent Republican Nancy Mace began running campaign ads accusing Andrews of engaging in "child abuse" by providing gender-affirming care and surgery to minors. In a statement, Andrews responded: "I knew politics would be ugly but I never thought I'd see the day when my own congresswoman would accuse me—a pediatrician for over a decade—of 'child abuse' […] I do not support gender-affirming surgery for anyone under 18—nor does my hospital perform those procedures. What I support is evidence-based medical care, with parental consent, for teens struggling with gender identity issues […] Her vicious and disgusting attacks against me and LGBTQ kids puts them at an increased risk of targeted attacks and self-harm behaviors." Andrews also said that because of the ads, she would begin taking unpaid leave from her job as a pediatrician at MUSC Children's Hospital and would increase her personal security while she considered legal action, citing threats made against doctors earlier that year, including a bomb threat against the Boston Children's Hospital.

In October, Andrews was endorsed by the editorial board of the Charleston City Paper.

Andrews lost the general election on November 8, receiving 42.5% of the vote to Mace's 56.5%. She called Mace to concede soon afterward, and Mace responded by thanking her for "stepping into the arena". Andrews said she was unsure where her political career would go next, but that her candidacy had encouraged more medical professionals to follow in her footsteps by running for elected office.

In April 2023, Andrews announced the creation of a PAC called "Their Future. Our Vote".

U.S. Senate
Elections
2026
On May 29, 2025, Andrews announced her candidacy for the United States Senate in 2026, aiming to unseat incumbent Republican senator Lindsey Graham. She said she decided to run for Senate after President Donald Trump appointed Robert F. Kennedy Jr. as Secretary of Health.

Andrews was endorsed by former Democratic National Committee chair Jaime Harrison, who ran against Graham in 2020. She was also endorsed by the 314 Action Fund, EMILYs List, the JAC, and Vote Mama.

On June 9, 2026, Andrews won the Democratic nomination, and was expected to face Graham. On July 11, Graham died, and two days later, his sister, Darline, was appointed to serve the remainder of his term. On August 25, Darline Graham won the South Carolina Republican Party nomination in a special primary runoff. Andrews faced Graham,  Constitution Party nominee Mark Hackett, Libertarian Kasie Whitener, and write-in candidate Catherine Fleming Bruce in the general election.

On November 3, Andrews won the general election, becoming the first woman to be elected to the Senate from South Carolina. The seat had been in Republican hands without interruption since 1964, when Strom Thurmond switched parties. Thurmond retired in 2003 and handed the seat to Lindsey Graham, who held it until his death.

Political positions
Andrews describes herself as a pragmatic, "radical centrist". Andrews supports abortion rights and universal background checks while differing with some of the Democratic Party's positions: she supports strengthened border security, term limits, a ban on congressional stock trading, and Trump's "no tax on tips" and "no tax on Social Security" proposals.

Healthcare affordability and Medicare
In an October 28, 2026, interview, Andrews said:

I know as a pediatrician that prevention is actually a cost-effective approach to building a health care system. So the more folks who have access to primary care doctors for early diagnosis and treatment of diseases, the less our entire health care system is going to cost. My plan includes a public option, and a public option doesn't mean the government is paying for someone's health care. A public option means that you or I have the option to purchase an affordable public plan if we choose.

In an August 2026 interview on NewsNation, Andrews said she considered Medicare for All (single-payer healthcare) "a policy from a few decades ago". She explained, "I'm a pragmatic person. When you work as a physician, you have to be a problem solver. You roll up your sleeves, you solve the problem in front of you, you work with whoever you need to work with, you don't let perfect be the enemy of good". 

Her priorities on the issue include reversing government healthcare cuts and increasing prescription drug affordability. These measures include, reversing the Medicaid cuts from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act restoring the enhanced Affordable Care Act tax provisions, and increasing prescription drug price negotiation under Medicare.

Reproductive healthcare and access
Andrews campaigned on a women's right to choose and increasing access to health services. On her campaign website she said, "These decisions belong to women and families, not to politicians". She vowed to restore the protections of Roe v. Wade, guarantee access to contraception and infertility care, and expand maternal health services, especially in rural communities. Finally, she mentions, "No woman should have to leave her state or risk her life to get medical care".

LGBTQ+ rights
In an October 5, 2026, Truth Social post, Trump used anti-LGBT slurs against Andrews, calling her "Annie Trannie". Andrews is not transgender. The post was widely condemned. Several days after Trump's post, Andrews released an ad saying that she opposed "biological men playing women's sports" and would not allow "gender surgery for minors", contradicting a 2022 X post denouncing the Save Women's Sports Act, a bill barring transgender athletes from girls' and women's sports teams in public schools, as "shameful", saying she would "always support trans youth".

Personal life
In 2011, Andrews married Charlie Andrews, a neurological critical care doctor at MUSC. They have three children. Andrews filed for divorce in August 2023, and it was finalized in 2024.
